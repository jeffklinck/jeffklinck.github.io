This paper develops a practical Canadian interpretation of the unified-ledger concept proposed by the Bank for International Settlements.

## Design premise

A common programmable infrastructure could allow tokenized deposits, central-bank settlement assets, and regulated financial instruments to interact without collapsing their legal or institutional distinctions.

## Questions the paper addresses

- Which assets and institutions should participate first?
- Where should programmability live?
- How should privacy, operational control, and governance be divided?
- What migration path avoids a brittle, all-at-once replacement of existing rails?

The emphasis is implementation: a useful blueprint must specify not only the target architecture, but also a credible sequence for reaching it.
