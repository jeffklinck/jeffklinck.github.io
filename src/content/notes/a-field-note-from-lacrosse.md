Good defence looks reactive from the stands. Up close, most of the work happens earlier: recognizing a shape, communicating the next rotation, and moving before the obvious threat develops.

## Communication is part of the system

A team cannot rely on every player seeing the same thing. Short, repeated calls create a shared model of the field. The vocabulary is useful because it is limited; everyone knows what each word commits them to doing next.

There is a parallel here with distributed systems. Coordination improves when messages are legible, responsibilities are explicit, and the system remains useful even when one participant has incomplete information.

## Preparation creates time

The fastest decision is often the one partly made in advance. Film, repetition, and agreed rules reduce the number of choices that have to be invented under pressure.

That does not eliminate improvisation. It gives improvisation a stable base.
